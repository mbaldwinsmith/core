# Course format and session method

Run 24 weekly sessions of 90 minutes, ideally in four blocks of six with breaks between blocks. Allow another 15 minutes for optional conversation afterwards. Groups of roughly 6–12 are a planning assumption, not a requirement. Reading between sessions is optional and normally takes no more than ten minutes. The framework's lists are writing briefs for leaders; learners receive a much shorter handout.

| Element | Minutes | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Encounter | 10 | Welcome, recap and an optional brief prayer or observation of an object or image |
| Question | 10 | One recognisable human question and space for participants' initial responses |
| Jesus | 10 | Read the selected biblical passage aloud and explain its context |
| Discovery | 20 | Teach the three essential ideas, with a pause halfway for clarification |
| Depth | 15 | Read one short historical quotation and address the three prepared FAQs |
| Practice | 15 | Complete the specified activity in pairs, alone or through discussion |
| Return and sending | 10 | Check understanding, offer one weekly practice and close with prayer |

Every session below specifies an observable outcome, essential teaching, Scripture and Catechism references, a historical voice, three FAQs with starter answers, a 15-minute activity, an exit check and an optional weekly practice. This makes each a commissionable writing brief. The quotation identifiers Q1–Q15 refer to the actual quotations in G4. CCC means Catechism of the Catholic Church; the numbers identify paragraphs, not pages. The listed ranges are reference material for writers, not compulsory learner reading.

**Essential and deeper learning.** Teach the three numbered ideas in each session first. Define no more than three new specialist words in the main learner handout. Put historical disputes, additional quotations and specialist explanations in a clearly optional further-reading page. A facilitator may say, “I need to check that,” and return with a sourced answer. Use the same approach for questions whose answers depend on a person's circumstances.

**Materials and preparation.** All sessions need the free learner sheet, a Bible or supplied passage references, paper and pens; speaking or listening can replace writing. Leaders read the named CCC paragraphs and quotation context, prepare a plain-language explanation and check the activity in advance. Additional objects are named below and may always be replaced by a printed description. A trained lead and a second helper are useful where pastoral needs or accessibility require them.

**Alternative pacing.** In a 60-minute meeting use 5 minutes welcome, 5 question, 10 Scripture, 15 teaching, 10 quotation and FAQs, 10 practice, 5 sending. Offer further questions afterwards. If a group needs more time, split the session; never omit entire sacraments or the moral life to meet an arbitrary deadline. A six-session introduction may signpost the full course but should not be presented as equivalent formation.
