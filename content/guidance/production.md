# Production plan and pilot

Start with a modest pilot before writing or printing the entire course. The stages below are a proposed sequence, not a promised timetable. Estimate effort and set dates once collaborators and approval routes are known; ecclesial review and permissions have their own timescales. The named roles may be fulfilled by a small team, but doctrinal review should involve someone other than the main writer.

| Stage | Owner | Work and concrete output | Completion test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Scope and review route | Mark and a parish catechetical adviser | Confirm adult audience, rite, intended use, course outcome and contact with the ordinary's office | Written brief and publication-review route agreed |
| 2 Authoring pattern | Mark | Draft Sessions 01, 03, 10 and 16 as complete learner and leader packs | All four have timed plans, FAQ answers, activities and source entries |
| 3 Content and rights check | Qualified reviewer and rights editor | Check doctrine, quotation wording and proposed reusable editions; identify permissions needed | No unresolved doctrinal correction; no unlabelled third-party material |
| 4 Accessibility check | Accessibility tester and two representative readers | Try print and digital files, glossary, audio or transcript and alternative activities | Readers can find the main point and complete the practical task without inaccessible steps |
| 5 Supervised small pilot | Facilitator and helper | Trial the four selected sessions with roughly 6–10 adults, subject to the agreed ecclesial review arrangements | Actual timings logged; misunderstandings and pastoral concerns recorded without unnecessary personal data |
| 6 Revise and complete | Mark and reviewers | Fix pilot issues, then write the other sessions in batches of four | Each batch meets the same learning and review gates |
| 7 Publication readiness | Publisher and competent ecclesiastical contacts | Complete permissions, required approval process, accessible exports and stable versioning | Release checklist signed off; approval claims match what has actually been granted |
| 8 Print proof and release | Publisher and printer | Proof a learner book and leader book against the free version | Text parity, legibility, binding and total delivered cost checked before ordering |

**Pilot measures.** After each session ask participants to explain the central idea in one sentence, identify one unanswered question and say whether the practice was feasible. Invite feedback on clarity and welcome without soliciting private religious or medical history. Record where the meeting ran over time. With a small pilot, treat numbers as signals rather than evidence of broad effectiveness. A useful initial gate is that at least four of five participants who choose to answer can explain the central distinction, with all substantive doctrinal misunderstandings prompting review.

**First four writing commissions.** Session 01 tests welcome and tone; Session 03 tests the explanation of difficult doctrine; Session 10 tests ecumenical and devotional distinctions; Session 16 tests sacramental precision. If only one full session can be written first, choose Session 01 and build the reusable authoring pattern from it. A parish should not use isolated pilot meetings as sufficient sacramental preparation.

**Print economics.** Obtain quotes for one proof and several small quantities, then calculate net receipts minus printing, packing, payment fees, postage, replacements and any applicable taxes. Do not set prices or order substantial stock from unverified estimates. Consider print on demand or parish pre-orders after a proof has been approved. All print content should identify the free digital equivalent and the edition date.

**Release checklist.** Every session has a plain-language outcome, three essential ideas, defined terms, checked Scripture and CCC references, a verified quotation, three FAQ answers, a timed activity, an answer key, an accessible alternative and a next step. Separately check theological review, rights, the applicable ecclesial approval, document accessibility and print proofing. Track changes publicly and review any time-sensitive local guidance before each course run.
