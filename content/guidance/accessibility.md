# Accessibility and facilitator support

Write each main learner sheet in plain English, normally 600–900 words with short paragraphs, three essential points, three FAQs and space for a question. The completed session can have a 1200–1800-word leader script plus optional background. These are production targets, not a requirement to read everything aloud. Explain a technical word at first use and repeat its definition when it returns after several weeks.

Provide selectable text, a structured HTML version, a tagged PDF or accessible equivalent, an audio reading or transcript, and a large-print version. Test actual files with keyboard navigation and a screen reader rather than relying on a claimed standard. Use at least 12-point print body text, generous spacing and high contrast; large print starts at 16–18 points, adjusted to readers' needs. Captions and transcripts are required for teaching videos. Images need meaningful alternative text or captions where they convey content.

Offer listening, speaking, writing or quiet observation as equivalent ways of taking part. Do not require reading aloud, closed eyes, kneeling, disclosure of conversion stories or public explanations of marital, sexual or medical circumstances. Optional activities must include a seated and remote alternative where practical. Let people take breaks and leave without explaining why. Apply the host parish's safeguarding arrangements; never describe group discussion as absolutely confidential.

**Working glossary.** A creed is a shared statement of faith. Revelation is God's making himself known. Tradition is the handing on of apostolic faith; a custom is a particular practice. Grace is God's freely given help and gift of participation in his life. A mystery is a divine reality genuinely revealed but not exhausted by our understanding. A sacrament is an effective sign of grace instituted by Christ and entrusted to the Church. Liturgy is the Church's public worship. A diocese is a local Church entrusted to a bishop. The Magisterium is the Church's living teaching office. Dogma is a truth the Church definitively proposes as divinely revealed. Veneration is honour; adoration is worship owed to God. A charism is a gift given for service. Mystagogy means growing in understanding of the mysteries through the worship and life into which one has been initiated.

**Facilitator pack.** Include a timed plan, plain-language answer key, quotation context, relevant CCC passages, accessibility options, sensitive-topic notes and local referral blanks. Before a meeting, check any local statements about sacramental preparation, Mass schedules, fasting exceptions or chaplaincy contacts. Do not resolve complex canonical questions through a general FAQ.

**Learning checks.** Use low-pressure retrieval after sessions 6, 12, 18 and 24: explain one central teaching, correct one common misconception and choose one appropriate practical step. Leaders use an answer key and invite further questions. Do not grade prayer, confession attendance, emotional disclosures or personal holiness.
