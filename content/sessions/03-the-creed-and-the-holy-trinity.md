# The Creed and the Holy Trinity

## Begin with a question

Who is the God Christians confess? Participants can identify the Creed's Trinitarian structure and explain one God in three distinct divine Persons without describing three gods or three disguises.

## Explore the faith

1. The Apostles' and Nicene Constantinopolitan Creeds summarise the Church's faith; the latter developed through the fourth-century councils.

2. Father, Son and Holy Spirit are coequal and coeternal; each is fully the one God, not a third of God.

3. We are invited by grace into communion with the Trinity. “Person” here is not simply the modern idea of three independent individuals. Divine actions towards creation are inseparable, although particular works are appropriately associated with a Person.

## Read and discover

Matthew 3:13–17; Matthew 28:19; CCC 185–197, 232–267; Q5 Basil on the Spirit's gifts. References R4 and the Creed text linked through R0.

## Questions worth asking

### Is this a contradiction?

The claim is one divine nature and three Persons, not one and three in the same respect.

### Is Jesus a lesser god?

No; the Son is eternally God, of the same divine nature as the Father.

### Can water or a shamrock explain it?

Such comparisons can mislead by suggesting changing forms or parts; state their limits and prefer the baptismal words.

## Reflect and practise

Give each pair three short descriptions: three gods, one actor wearing masks, and one God in three Persons. Use 5 minutes to sort, 5 to explain and 5 to connect the Creed with Baptism. Expected output: corrected statements. Exit check: explain why “God has three parts” fails.

## Carry it into the week

Pray the sign of the cross slowly, or observe its words if not ready to pray.
