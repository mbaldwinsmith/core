# Catholic social teaching and works of mercy

## Begin with a question

What does love require in public life? Participants can explain dignity, solidarity, subsidiarity and the common good, and plan a modest action with affected people's input.

## Explore the faith

1. Human dignity applies from conception to natural death and includes the vulnerable, poor, disabled, migrant and excluded.

2. Solidarity means responsibility for one another; subsidiarity protects the proper agency of persons and smaller communities while requiring larger bodies to help when needed. The common good concerns conditions that allow people and communities to flourish.

3. Works of mercy, just treatment of workers, peace and care for creation express discipleship. Distinguish binding moral principles from prudential disagreement about policies; the faith cannot be identified with one political party.

## Read and discover

Matthew 25:31–46; Luke 10:25–37; James 2:14–17; CCC 1877–1948, 2401–2463; Deus caritas est 25; Q14 Francis as the invitation to embody received mercy. References R27, R2.

## Questions worth asking

### Is charity enough if systems are unjust?

Immediate help and work for justice belong together.

### Does subsidiarity mean governments should never act?

No; larger bodies have a duty to support and act where lower levels cannot adequately protect the common good.

### Must Catholics agree on every policy?

No; they must respect moral principles, evidence and the dignity of those affected while discerning practical means.

## Facilitator preparation

Before the meeting, obtain a brief from an existing local organisation describing a need and the help it actually welcomes. Use an anonymised fictional case if no partner is available.

## Reflect and practise

Spend 5 minutes understanding the prearranged brief from the local organisation, 5 testing a proposed response against the four principles and 5 assigning a small next step. Expected output: a one-page plan with an owner, a consent step and a realistic date. Exit check: show how solidarity and subsidiarity can support one another.

## Carry it into the week

Carry out or arrange one agreed act of mercy. Check transport, accessibility, safeguarding and consent with the host organisation; no photographs or stories are shared without permission.
