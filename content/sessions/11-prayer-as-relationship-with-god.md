# Prayer as relationship with God

## Begin with a question

How can I begin praying honestly? Participants can try two forms of prayer and make a small, sustainable plan without equating success with a feeling.

## Explore the faith

1. Prayer responds to God's initiative and includes the whole person.

2. Blessing and adoration, petition, intercession, thanksgiving and praise shape Christian prayer; vocal prayer, meditation and contemplation are related expressions.

3. Distraction and dryness need patience rather than performance anxiety. Contemplation is a gift; no technique compels God or guarantees an experience.

## Read and discover

Luke 11:1–13; Romans 8:26–27; CCC 2558–2565, 2623–2649, 2700–2724; Q8 Thérèse and Q10 Teresa of Ávila. References R14, R15.

## Questions worth asking

### Are set prayers less authentic?

No; inherited words can express and educate genuine prayer.

### What if my attention wanders?

Return gently; the discovery of distraction can itself be a moment of turning back.

### What if prayer worsens distress?

Pause or change the exercise and seek suitable pastoral or professional support; this course does not prescribe treatment or demanding spiritual practices.

## Reflect and practise

Offer 3 minutes of a short spoken prayer, 3 of silent attention to a Gospel phrase, 4 for quiet reflection and 5 for making a two-minute daily plan. Listening with eyes open or simply observing is welcome. Expected output: a chosen prayer form and time. Exit check: explain why dryness is not necessarily failure.

## Carry it into the week

Try the two-minute practice on three days and note what helps. Offer the Jesus Prayer, a psalm or thanksgiving as options, without prescribing breathing methods or extended repetition.
