# Conscience the commandments and the Beatitudes

## Begin with a question

How does grace shape everyday choices? Participants can use a basic moral reasoning process, describe conscience and connect commandments with the Beatitudes and virtue.

## Explore the faith

1. Christian morality responds to grace and aims at love of God and neighbour. The commandments protect these relationships; the Beatitudes show the shape of life in Christ.

2. Conscience makes a practical judgment about a concrete choice. A person must follow its certain judgment and also take responsibility for forming it through truth, prayer, reason, Scripture and the Church's teaching. Error may be culpable or nonculpable; absence of personal guilt does not make an objectively wrongful act good.

3. Ask what is being chosen, why and in what circumstances. A good intention does not make an intrinsically wrongful choice good. Distinguish the act, the person's responsibility and their enduring dignity.

## Manageable teaching sequence

Within the 20-minute Discovery, use 4 minutes for the commandments map, 4 for the Beatitudes, 6 for conscience and 6 for the practical decision process. These four-minute segments provide orientation, not exhaustive treatment. Grace has already been introduced in Session 04, growth in holiness and the fruits of the Spirit in Session 06, prayer in Sessions 11–12, culpability in Session 18 and marriage in Session 21; recap these rather than introducing every field afresh. Name faith, hope and charity and the four cardinal virtues on a reference sheet, with definitions, rather than attempting seven additional mini-lectures. For a group without this foundation, split Session 22 into two 90-minute meetings: 22A commandments and Beatitudes, 22B conscience and choices. This gives 25 meetings while retaining the same 24 numbered topics.

## Commissioned commandments map

Use these short paraphrases as headings, with the approved Catechism text available to the writer: 1 worship God alone; 2 honour God's name; 3 keep the Lord's day; 4 honour parents and family responsibilities; 5 protect human life; 6 live sexual fidelity and chastity; 7 respect others' goods and economic justice; 8 tell the truth and protect a neighbour's reputation; 9 cultivate purity of heart in sexual desire; 10 resist envy and disordered desire for possessions. Present 1–3 under love of God and 4–10 under love of neighbour, without claiming the two loves can be separated. Read all eight Beatitudes in Matthew 5:3–12; highlight mercy, peacemaking and purity of heart as examples of positive discipleship, not as a replacement list of commandments. The learner output is one concrete link between a commandment, a Beatitude and an act of love.

## Read and discover

Exodus 20:1–17; Matthew 5:1–12; Mark 12:28–34; CCC 1716–1729, 1750–1756, 1776–1802, 1803–1829, 2041–2043, 2052–2082; Q15 Leo the Great on dignity. References R26 and R0.

## Questions worth asking

### Is conscience whatever I feel?

No; it involves an honest judgment that should be informed and examined.

### Do rules earn salvation?

No; grace comes first, and moral growth is our enabled response.

### What about sexuality or contested teachings?

Explain the Church's teaching clearly and compassionately, distinguishing persons from acts and basic dignity from moral approval; provide optional, reviewed learning and private pastoral guidance without humiliating participants.

## Reflect and practise

Spend 5 minutes mapping the commandments to love of God and neighbour, 5 analysing an invented workplace dishonesty case and 5 proposing a virtuous alternative. Expected output: act, intention, circumstances and next step. Exit check: explain why a helpful motive does not justify every method.

## Carry it into the week

Practise one virtue in one specific decision. The writer commissions further-learning sheets on life ethics, sexuality, money, truthfulness and digital behaviour; these are free, not a paid extension.

## Minimum briefs for the five further-learning sheets

Each receives a plain-language summary, two FAQs, one everyday case, a practical next step and a qualified doctrinal review. Distribute them as a free reference pack; they are not five extra talks to squeeze into this meeting.

## Practical Church precepts map

The free leader and learner pack includes a one-page reference to CCC 2041–2043: Sunday and holy-day Mass with legitimate excuses (Session 17); annual confession of grave sins as applicable (Session 18); annual Communion, ordinarily in the Easter season, with proper preparation (Session 16); prescribed fasting and abstinence, with age limits, exemptions and local rules verified for the course's location; and material support for the Church according to one's ability. These are minimum practices within a life of love, not a complete definition of holiness. The local supplement supplies the current calendar and fasting rules; this framework does not invent universal dates or treat voluntary devotions as obligations.
