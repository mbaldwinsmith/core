# The Our Father and a rhythm of prayer

## Begin with a question

How does Jesus teach us to pray? Participants can explain the Lord's Prayer's seven petitions and build a realistic rhythm of personal and communal prayer.

## Explore the faith

1. We pray to the Father with the Son in the Spirit; “our” includes other people and their needs.

2. The petitions move through God's name, kingdom and will to daily bread, forgiveness, temptation and deliverance. God does not tempt us to evil.

3. Scripture, the Liturgy of the Hours, Sunday worship and simple household prayer connect daily life to the Church's prayer. Devotional practices support rather than replace the sacraments.

## Read and discover

Matthew 6:5–15; CCC 2759–2865; Q11 Cyprian on common prayer. Reference R16 and Q11's source.

## Questions worth asking

### Must I use old-fashioned English?

Approved translations differ; explain the meaning and use the local approved text in common worship.

### Does forgiveness require returning to an unsafe relationship?

No; forgiveness does not remove the need for boundaries, justice or protection.

### Must I pray for hours?

No; start with a sustainable commitment appropriate to your responsibilities.

## Reflect and practise

Spend 7 minutes pairing the seven petitions with everyday concerns, 3 praying or listening to the prayer, and 5 creating a weekly rhythm. Expected output: a prayer plan with one realistic daily anchor and a communal option. Exit check: explain “daily bread” beyond one's own needs.

## Carry it into the week

Pray one petition slowly each day. Leaders prepare a large-print prayer sheet or audio alternative; reproduction permissions are checked before release.
