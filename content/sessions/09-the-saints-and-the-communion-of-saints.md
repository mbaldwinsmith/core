# The saints and the communion of saints

## Begin with a question

What does friendship across death mean? Participants can distinguish adoration of God, honour for saints and requests for intercession, and connect sanctity with ordinary life.

## Explore the faith

1. Communion includes sharing in holy things and communion among holy persons, on earth, in purification and in glory.

2. Christ is the one mediator; the prayers of his members participate in his mediation rather than rival it.

3. Canonised saints are recognised examples and intercessors, not flawless authorities on everything. Include women and men, lay and ordained, East and West, and different cultures; choose sourced biographies rather than embellished stories.

## Read and discover

Hebrews 12:1–2; Revelation 5:8; 1 Timothy 2:1–5; CCC 946–962, 2683–2684; Q8 Thérèse as an example of accessible holiness. Reference R12.

## Questions worth asking

### Why ask saints when I can pray to God?

You can and should pray directly to God; asking saints resembles asking fellow Christians to pray, within Christ's communion.

### Are statues idols?

Images may help remembrance and veneration; they are not gods or magical objects.

### Must I have a favourite saint?

No; particular friendships and devotions are optional.

## Reflect and practise

Spend 5 minutes comparing a short biography of Thérèse with a lay saint's, 5 identifying a realistic virtue and 5 distinguishing a reliable fact from an unsourced anecdote. Expected output: a small “virtue in ordinary life” plan. Exit check: explain the difference between honour and worship.

## Carry it into the week

Read a short, sourced saint's biography and choose one modest act of love. The writer commissions a geographically varied biography set with sources and image credits.
