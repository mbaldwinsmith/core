# The sacramental life and the liturgical year

## Begin with a question

Why does faith involve water, oil, bread and wine? Participants can name all seven sacraments, group them and distinguish a sacrament from a sacramental.

## Explore the faith

1. Sacraments are effective signs of grace instituted by Christ and entrusted to the Church. Christ acts through them; their efficacy does not rest on the minister's personal holiness.

2. Their fruits also involve the recipient's disposition; they are not mechanical magic. Baptism, Confirmation and Orders impart an enduring sacramental character and are not repeated.

3. Initiation, healing and service of communion organise the seven. Blessings and other sacramentals prepare us to receive grace but are distinct. The liturgical year forms us around Christ's mystery.

## Read and discover

John 1:14; Romans 6:3–4; CCC 1066–1134, 1163–1173, 1210–1212, 1667–1679; Q12, attributed to Cyril, on learning through experienced worship. Reference R17.

## Questions worth asking

### Why seven?

The Church recognises seven sacraments instituted by Christ, rather than treating every religious symbol as a sacrament.

### Is grace a substance in an object?

Grace is God's gift and action, not a measurable fluid.

### Are Eastern Catholic celebrations different?

Their rites differ while celebrating the same sacramental faith; avoid mistaking a Roman custom for the only Catholic practice.

## Reflect and practise

Spend 5 minutes sorting seven name cards, 5 distinguishing a sacrament from a blessed object and 5 placing major seasons on a simple calendar. Expected output: correct categories and a liturgical-year map. Exit check: give one reason a sacrament is more than a visual reminder.

## Carry it into the week

Notice the coming Sunday's season and one sign used in worship. Use descriptions of sacred objects; never stage a sacrament as a classroom simulation.
