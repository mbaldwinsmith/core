# Creation human dignity sin and grace

## Begin with a question

Why is a good world also wounded? Participants can distinguish created goodness, original sin and personal sin, and explain grace as God's freely given help and life.

## Explore the faith

1. God creates freely from nothing; every human being has inherent dignity. Creation and scientific accounts address different kinds of questions, though their relationship merits thoughtful discussion.

2. Original sin is an inherited deprivation of original holiness, not a personal act committed by an infant; human nature is wounded rather than wholly destroyed.

3. Grace precedes and enables our response. Salvation is God's gift, calling forth faith working through love; it cannot be purchased by accumulated good deeds.

## Read and discover

Genesis 1:26–31; Romans 5:12–21; Ephesians 2:8–10; CCC 279–324, 355–421, 1987–2016; Q4 Athanasius on sharing God's life. References R5 and R0.

## Questions worth asking

### Is the body bad?

No; bodily life belongs to God's good creation and Christian hope includes resurrection.

### Does illness prove someone sinned?

No; do not infer personal guilt from suffering or disability.

### Why does God allow evil?

Christian faith offers creation, freedom, the cross and resurrection as a framework of hope, not a complete explanation of every tragedy.

## Reflect and practise

Use 5 minutes to list signs of goodness and brokenness in an invented community, 5 to distinguish responsibility from misfortune and 5 to propose a grace-enabled response. Expected output: a case analysis without blaming sufferers. Exit check: explain gift and response together.

## Carry it into the week

Notice one gift and perform one small act of care. Personal experiences of trauma are never requested as examples.
