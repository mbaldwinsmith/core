# The Pope bishops and teaching authority

## Begin with a question

What is the Pope for? Participants can explain the Bishop of Rome's Petrine ministry, recognise the role of bishops and distinguish infallibility from sinlessness or universal expertise.

## Explore the faith

1. The Pope succeeds Peter and serves visible unity; bishops are successors of the apostles, with their own responsibility for their Churches.

2. The Magisterium is the Church's living teaching office, serving the received faith rather than inventing revelation.

3. Papal infallibility applies under defined conditions to definitive teaching on faith or morals for the whole Church; it does not cover every comment. Infallibility also involves the college of bishops and definitive ordinary and universal teaching. Non-definitive authoritative teaching still calls for religious submission of intellect and will; a private opinion is a different category.

## Read and discover

Matthew 16:13–19; Luke 22:31–32; John 21:15–17; CCC 880–896; Lumen gentium 22–25; Q7 Benedict XVI on the Pope's service. References R10, R11 and Q7's source.

## Questions worth asking

### Can a pope sin?

Yes; infallibility is not personal impeccability.

### Can he change the Gospel?

No; his ministry is bound to God's Word.

### Does every disagreement mean rejection of the Church?

Questions, difficulties and the authority of a particular statement require careful distinctions and appropriate guidance; do not reduce all teachings to optional opinion or all questions to disloyalty.
