# Anointing of the Sick and care at the end of life

## Begin with a question

How does Christ accompany serious illness? Participants can distinguish Anointing, pastoral care and Viaticum, and know how to request a priest in good time.

## Explore the faith

1. This sacrament is for baptised faithful whose health is seriously impaired through illness or old age; it is not reserved for the final minutes of life or applied indiscriminately to every minor illness.

2. A bishop or priest administers it through the Church's prayer and anointing with oil. It gives strength, peace, courage, union with Christ's passion and forgiveness of sins when the sick person cannot obtain it through Penance; bodily healing may be given if conducive to salvation.

3. Viaticum is Eucharistic Communion for the passage towards death; “last rites” may involve several distinct sacraments and prayers.

## Signs celebration and preparation

Describe prayer, laying on of hands and anointing of forehead and hands in the Latin rite; other rites differ. It can be repeated when serious illness recurs or the condition worsens. Contact the parish or hospital chaplain; do not wait unnecessarily. Individual eligibility, including mental illness, requires the priest's pastoral judgment rather than a course checklist. Sources: CCC 1499–1532 [R23].

## Read and discover

James 5:14–15; Mark 6:12–13; Q14 Francis, applied to Christ's compassionate presence, not a promise of medical cure.

## Questions worth asking

### Does requesting it mean giving up?

No; it accompanies treatment and hope.

### Will it guarantee recovery?

No; God's grace is not a guarantee of a particular medical outcome.

### What if the priest cannot arrive immediately?

Continue ordinary prayer and care, contact the appropriate chaplaincy route and follow medical advice; laypeople can pray but cannot administer this sacrament.

## Reflect and practise

Spend 5 minutes distinguishing three fictional care situations, 5 locating the parish and hospital chaplain routes and 5 composing a compassionate request. Expected output: a contact card, checked by the local organiser before distribution. Exit check: explain why Anointing and Viaticum are different.

## Carry it into the week

Offer a practical act of support with consent. No uninvited visits, promises of healing or classroom imitations of anointing.
