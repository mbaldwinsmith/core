# The Holy Spirit and Christian transformation

## Begin with a question

How does God act in us now? Participants can identify the Spirit as a divine Person, describe sanctification and distinguish a spiritual gift from proof of personal superiority.

## Explore the faith

1. The Spirit is Lord and giver of life, not an impersonal energy.

2. The Spirit unites us to Christ and forms the Church through Scripture, sacraments, prayer and charity.

3. Gifts are for service and require discernment; growth is recognised through charity and the fruits of the Spirit, not measured by dramatic experiences. Define sanctification as being made holy through God's grace.

## Read and discover

Acts 2:1–11; Romans 8:14–17; Galatians 5:22–25; CCC 683–747, 1830–1832; Q5 Basil. Reference R7.

## Questions worth asking

### Must I speak in tongues?

No; not all receive the same gifts.

### Does feeling nothing mean God is absent?

No; feelings vary and are not a sacramental validity test or a measure of God's love.

### Is every strong impression God's instruction?

No; test impressions against faith, reason, charity, responsibilities and wise counsel.

## Reflect and practise

Use 5 minutes to read the fruits of the Spirit, 5 to discuss an invented case of a gifted but unkind leader and 5 to identify a humble service response. Expected output: three sensible discernment questions. Exit check: name one ordinary way the Spirit acts.

## Carry it into the week

Ask for help to practise one fruit in a specific relationship. Leaders avoid diagnosing experiences or identifying distress as demonic activity.
