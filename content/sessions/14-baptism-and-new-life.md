# Baptism and new life

## Begin with a question

What does it mean to be born into Christ? Participants can describe the essential celebration, its effects and how an adult or family begins appropriate parish preparation.

## Explore the faith

1. Baptism joins us to Christ's death and resurrection, forgives original and personal sin, grants new life and incorporates us into the Church.

2. The essential sign is washing with real water with the Trinitarian baptismal words and the intention to do what the Church does. Ordinary ministers are bishops, priests and deacons in the Latin Church; in necessity anyone with the required intention can baptise.

3. A person not already baptised can receive it. Infant Baptism witnesses to grace's priority and requires subsequent formation; adults prepare through faith, conversion and the Church's initiation process.

## Signs celebration and preparation

Explain water and the Trinitarian words first; then the associated anointings, white garment, candle and baptismal promises. Baptism gives an enduring seal, so a valid Christian Baptism is not repeated. Uncertainty about an earlier Baptism belongs with the parish, not a group vote. Eastern initiation commonly joins Baptism, Chrismation and Eucharist together. Sources: CCC 1213–1284 [R18].

## Read and discover

Matthew 28:18–20; Romans 6:3–11; Q12, attributed to Cyril, used as a historical witness to formation around Baptism, not as a modern ritual manual.

## Questions worth asking

### Why baptise infants?

God's gift precedes mature understanding, while parents and community undertake formation in faith.

### Does Baptism remove every struggle?

No; weakness, suffering and temptation remain.

### What about those who die without Baptism?

Teach its necessity alongside Baptism of blood, desire and God's freedom; the Church entrusts unbaptised infants to God's mercy and has hope for their salvation, rather than asserting their condemnation.

## Reflect and practise

Spend 5 minutes identifying the font and signs through a picture or visit, 5 tracing “gift and lifelong growth,” and 5 planning an appropriate parish enquiry. Expected output: an annotated signs sheet and a preparation contact. Exit check: distinguish Baptism's effects from later learning.

## Carry it into the week

Reflect on baptismal promises or discuss a Baptism enquiry privately. No class exercise pours water while reciting a baptismal formula over someone.
