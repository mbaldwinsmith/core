# The Eucharist and the real presence

## Begin with a question

What does the Church believe it receives in Holy Communion? Participants can explain real presence, sacrifice and communion, and identify the usual preparation for receiving.

## Explore the faith

1. The Eucharist makes sacramentally present Christ's one sacrifice; he is not crucified again.

2. By consecration the whole substance of bread and wine becomes Christ's body and blood while their appearances remain. Transubstantiation names this real change; the whole living Christ is present under either species.

3. Communion unites us with Christ and his body, the Church, and commits us to charity. Christ is present in several ways in the liturgy; his Eucharistic presence is unique.

## Signs celebration and preparation

A validly ordained bishop or priest consecrates. The celebration uses wheat bread and natural grape wine according to the Church's requirements; lay ministers may distribute Communion but do not consecrate. Ordinarily Catholic recipients must be properly prepared and disposed. Someone conscious of grave sin should receive sacramental absolution first, subject to the Church's stated exceptional conditions. For the Latin Church explain the usual one-hour fast before reception, with water and medicine excepted; the elderly, sick and those caring for them have the exemptions stated in canon 919. Eastern fasting discipline differs. Refer a person conscious of grave sin to the priest: canon 916 specifies the exceptional conjunction of grave reason, no opportunity to confess, and perfect contrition including the intention to confess as soon as possible. Attendance never creates an obligation to receive. Explain the Church’s precept to receive Communion at least annually, ordinarily during the Easter season, after preparation and subject to the conditions and exceptions stated in canon 920; it does not authorise unprepared reception. Sources: CCC 1322–1419, especially 1374–1377 and 1385–1390 [R20].

## Read and discover

John 6:51–58; 1 Corinthians 10:16–17; 11:23–29; Q13 Justin, with its second-century context.

## Questions worth asking

### Is it only a symbol?

It is a sacramental sign that truly contains Christ, not merely a reminder.

### Is Jesus divided when the host is broken?

No; Christ is whole under each species and each part.

### Can every attendee receive?

No; explain Catholic discipline respectfully, acknowledge narrowly defined exceptions through competent ministers and offer a clear way to remain seated without embarrassment.

Expected output: a respectful visitor explanation. Exit check: explain why receiving under one species does not mean receiving half of Christ.
