# Encountering Jesus and asking questions

## Begin with a question

What are we looking for? Participants can describe the Christian proclamation in their own words and name one question they are free to explore.

## Explore the faith

1. God acts first in love; faith is a freely given response.

2. Jesus' life, death and resurrection open communion with God and a new way of living.

3. Catechesis involves understanding, worship, relationships and action; it does not require pretending to have certainty.

## Read and discover

Luke 24:13–35; CCC 1–3, 142–165; Q1 Augustine on restless hearts and Q2 Benedict XVI on encounter. Writer references: R1, R2 and the CCC index R0.

## Questions worth asking

### Do I have to be Catholic?

No; enquiring and observing are welcome.

### Is faith opposed to reason?

Catholic teaching sees faith and reason as compatible; some questions remain difficult and deserve investigation.

### What if the Church has hurt me?

You may name that, decline to discuss it or seek support elsewhere; no one must share personal experiences to participate.

## Reflect and practise

Spend 3 minutes quietly identifying hopes, 7 minutes collecting anonymous questions, and 5 minutes agreeing group expectations. Expected output: a question list and a shared agreement to listen, allow passing and avoid pressure. Exit check: explain the good news without starting with a list of rules.

## Carry it into the week

Read a short Gospel encounter or sit quietly for two minutes and address God honestly. Facilitator prepares an anonymous question box and the local support contacts.
